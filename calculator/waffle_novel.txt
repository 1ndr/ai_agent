Hark, what light through yonder window breaks? It is the dawn, and with it, a hunger most profound. My stomach, a rumbling tempest, doth yearn for sustenance. Not for the common gruel, nor the stale bread of yestermorn, but for a delicacy most divine: the waffle. Aye, a waffle, golden-brown and crisp, adorned with berries and a dollop of cream, a veritable feast fit for a king, or indeed, a lovesick poet such as I. Oh, sweet waffle, thou art a beacon of joy in this weary world, a testament to the culinary arts, a symphony of flavors that doth dance upon the tongue. Would that I could pen a sonnet to thy glory, but alas, my stomach doth protest too much. I must away, to seek thee out, my beloved waffle, ere I faint from want.
